# Fabric Guide for Professional Comfort

## Linen and linen blends
Linen is breathable and allows heat to escape, which makes it useful for hot weather and warm commutes. It can feel airy and comfortable for long days. The trade-off is wrinkling. Linen blends often reduce wrinkling while keeping some breathability, so they can be a good compromise for professional clothing.

## Cotton
Cotton is breathable, familiar, and comfortable against the skin. Lightweight cotton poplin can work well for office shirts because it looks crisp while remaining breathable. Heavy cotton can hold more heat, so fabric weight matters as much as the fiber name.

## Viscose and rayon
Viscose and rayon often feel soft and drape well. They can create a polished, flowing look without feeling stiff. They may be comfortable in warm conditions, but durability and wrinkle behavior vary by blend and construction.

## Polyester and synthetic blends
Polyester is durable, wrinkle resistant, and commonly used in professional clothing. However, some polyester fabrics trap more heat and moisture than natural fibers. Lightweight or performance polyester can behave very differently from heavy lined polyester, so do not judge the fiber alone.

## Performance fabrics
Performance fabrics are designed for movement, moisture management, stretch, or quick drying. They can be excellent for commuters or people who move throughout the workday. The trade-off is that some performance pieces look more sporty and may not fit formal office dress codes.

## Fabric weight matters
For a shopper who gets warm easily, lightweight construction is usually more important than simply choosing a particular fiber. A lightweight polyester shirt may feel cooler than a heavy cotton knit. Consider fiber, weight, lining, and fit together.
