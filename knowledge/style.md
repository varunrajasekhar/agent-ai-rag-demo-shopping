# Professional Style Guide

## Business formal
Business formal clothing is structured and conservative. Tailored blazers, dress trousers, formal shirts, and polished shoes are common. Structure creates a sharp appearance, but highly structured and lined garments can feel less comfortable during long days or warm commutes.

## Business casual
Business casual balances professionalism and comfort. Straight or relaxed trousers, polished shirts, knit polos, unstructured blazers, and clean minimal pieces can work well. This category gives shoppers more flexibility to choose breathable fabrics and comfortable fits.

## Smart casual
Smart casual is less formal than business casual but still intentional and polished. Clean polos, minimal trousers, soft layers, and refined casual pieces may work well in flexible workplaces. Always consider the specific workplace because a sporty item may be acceptable in one office and too casual in another.

## Looking polished without feeling stiff
A professional outfit does not always require maximum structure. Clean lines, good drape, neutral or coordinated colors, and appropriate fit can create a polished look while allowing relaxed construction. An unstructured blazer or flowing trouser can look professional without feeling restrictive.

## Hot commute to office
For someone who gets warm during a commute, prioritize lightweight layers that can be removed, breathable or moisture-managing fabrics, and fits that allow airflow. Avoid assuming that the most formal item is automatically the best choice. The goal is to arrive comfortable while still meeting the workplace dress standard.
