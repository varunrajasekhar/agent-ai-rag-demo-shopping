# Fit and Comfort Guide

## Relaxed fit
Relaxed fits provide more room through the body or leg and can improve airflow and freedom of movement. They are useful for long workdays, commuting, and sitting for extended periods. A relaxed fit can still look professional when the garment has clean lines and intentional proportions.

## Straight fit
Straight-leg trousers and regular-fit tops provide a middle ground between fitted and oversized clothing. They are often easy to style professionally and may remain comfortable for a wide range of body types and work environments.

## Wide-leg fit
Wide-leg trousers provide movement and airflow. Pleats and drape can make them look polished. However, fabric choice matters: a wide-leg synthetic trouser may still feel warmer than a breathable natural-fiber option.

## Slim and fitted styles
Slim or fitted garments can create a sharp silhouette, but they may reduce airflow and become uncomfortable during long periods of sitting or in warm weather. Stretch can improve movement, but stretch does not necessarily make a garment cooler.

## Tailored and structured fit
Tailoring can communicate professionalism, especially in formal workplaces. Structured garments may use lining, interfacing, or heavier materials. These features can improve shape but also add warmth and reduce flexibility.

## Comfort is multi-factor
Comfort is not determined by fit alone. Consider fit together with fabric, weight, stretch, lining, activity level, temperature, and how long the garment will be worn.
